# To Ritu Deshpande

1 October 2026

Do not manage this on accuracy. Paying every claim already scores 98.7% on the investigated history and stops zero fraud. In June, paying everyone scored 96.9%. The queue that actually caught fraud scored 95.6%. The board's 97% is what you get by looking at nothing.

Farhan's desk can see forty claims a month. That is the constraint. The number to put in front of the board is rupees stopped per claim checked.

**It is not the new partners as a class.** An outlet joined in the last year has a 2.2% confirmed-fraud rate. An older outlet has 1.1%. Most of the partners you added for the smaller cities are fine, and you need them. Ten outlets account for 89 of the 141 confirmed frauds. The expensive pattern through April sat in older franchises: Hyderabad SP3207, Mysuru SP3376, Bhopal SP3095, Jaipur SP3103, Hubballi SP3350. They went quiet in May and June.

**The May rule opened a second hole.** From 1 May, claims under Rs 2,000 are paid without an inspection. Confirmed fraud went from 9 in April to 14 in May and 22 in June, almost all under that line, several filed at exactly Rs 1,995. Those claims came from a short list, not from the new network: Pune SP3160, Jaipur SP3232, Hubballi SP3318, Chennai SP3129, Warangal SP3319, and a handful of others. Ninety claims in May and June were filed between Rs 1,990 and Rs 1,999. Eight of those were confirmed fraud. The July–September file has 133 claims in that same band.

**What forty checks did, on the one month we could score without peeking.** June was scored using only what was known at the end of May. The top forty caught 15 of 22 confirmed frauds: Rs 21,040 of Rs 45,673. Twenty-five genuine customers would have been held, about Rs 9,500 of goodwill. Net about Rs 11,500. That is about Rs 530 of fraud stopped per claim checked, and about Rs 290 after goodwill. Resampling May and rescoring June, the catch was between 11 and 15, median 14. Treat 15 as a point, not a promise.

A claim is worth a slot when the chance it is fraud, times the rupees, is greater than the Rs 380 goodwill of delaying a genuine repair. On the July–September file that test flags 38, 40 and 35 claims. That is a full desk. We do not yet know how many of those are fraud.

**Next week**

1. Take these outlets off auto-approval, and no others: SP3160 Pune, SP3232 Jaipur, SP3318 Hubballi, SP3129 Chennai, SP3319 Warangal, SP3118 Pune, SP3286 Mysuru. Leave the rest of the new network on the May rule.
2. Keep the older franchises above on a watch, not a ban. Their next large repair will keep surfacing until a run of clean investigations brings their rate down. Holding a genuine one still costs Rs 380.
3. Give the forty slots to the screen, highest rupees at stake first. Do not ask the model for a yes at 97% accuracy. That answer is "pay them all."
4. Ask Tanmay to export open investigations as blank. The old Zoho file stored unfinished cases as not-fraud, so some 2025 "clears" are still open.

**What this will miss.** The first claims from an outlet we have never caught, and a single large repair from a quiet one. June had one of those: Rs 17,866 at Coimbatore SP3275, sixty percent of a robot vacuum, no prior fraud on that outlet. The queue left it. There are about ten to fifteen repairs a month over Rs 8,000 and over half the list price, and about one in ten of those was fraud before May. I would not give them all forty slots. I would pull any one claim over Rs 15,000 even when the outlet looks clean.
